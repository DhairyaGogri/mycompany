UK policy framework for responsible digital delivery.

1. Data Protection and Privacy Policy

Policy statement

Personal information must be processed lawfully, fairly, transparently and only for defined purposes. Processing must be minimised, accurate, retained no longer than necessary and kept secure.

Controls

  • Map personal data, purposes, lawful bases, categories, recipients, transfers and retention before processing.
  • Keep a record of processing activities and processor agreements where applicable.
  • Complete Data Protection Impact Assessments for high-risk or new monitoring, profiling or sensitive processing.
  • Use privacy notices, data-sharing agreements and confidentiality controls appropriate to each purpose.
  • Use data-subject rights procedures for access, correction, erasure, objection and restriction requests.
  • Review ICO registration and reporting duties. Personal data breaches must be assessed promptly.

Retention and security

Each record set must have an approved retention period and secure deletion method. Information must not be retained solely because it is convenient. UK GDPR data breach notification is considered without undue delay and within 72 hours where required.

Responsibility

The director is accountable. Staff and contractors must report accidental disclosure, misconfiguration or subject-rights requests immediately.

Evidence

Records of processing, lawful-basis decisions, DPIAs, processor reviews, rights requests, breach assessments, staff training and retention actions.

2. Information Security Policy

Policy statement

Information and systems must receive protection proportionate to confidentiality, integrity and availability risks, using NCSC Cyber Essentials and NCSC secure-design principles where appropriate.

Minimum controls

  • Unique accounts; MFA for email, source code, cloud, finance and administration.
  • Least-privilege access, joins/moves/leavers checks and quarterly access reviews.
  • Supported operating systems and applications, automatic updates, malware protection and timely patching.
  • Full-disk encryption for laptops and devices, secure screen lock and strong passphrases or password managers.
  • Separate personal and business accounts; no secrets, passwords or keys in source code or public sites.
  • Approved cloud repositories, secure configuration, backups and tested restoration.
  • Contractor and supplier access limited to need, time-bounded and reviewed.

Security development

Threat modelling, secure coding, dependency management, peer review, validation, secrets management and release checks apply to software work. No certification such as Cyber Essentials or ISO 27001 may be claimed unless valid evidence exists.

Exceptions and breaches

Exceptions require a recorded owner, risk, expiry date and compensating controls. Security incidents are reported immediately to the director.

Evidence

Device inventory, patch reports, access lists, MFA evidence, backup logs, restoration tests, vulnerability reviews and supplier assessments.

3. Cyber Incident Response Policy

Procedure

  1. Detect and report: preserve alerts, timestamps and affected accounts or systems.
  2. Assess severity: identify personal data, operational impact, ransomware risk and possible data loss.
  3. Contain: isolate affected devices, suspend compromised accounts and block malicious access.
  4. Investigate: collect evidence safely and maintain a contemporaneous incident timeline.
  5. Notify: escalate client or buyer requirements, ICO duties and law-enforcement matters as advised.
  6. Recover: restore from verified backups, reset credentials and monitor for recurrence.
  7. Learn: conduct a post-incident review and track corrective actions to completion.

Escalation

The director has authority to isolate systems and engage technical or legal support. Do not alter evidence or make misleading public statements.

Evidence

Incident register, timeline, action log, evidence inventory, notifications, restoration proof and lessons-learned record.

4. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Policy

Requirements

The company must identify critical activities, recovery priorities, maximum acceptable outages, key suppliers and alternate working arrangements.

  • Maintain and test encrypted backups independently of primary accounts where practicable.
  • Document account recovery, device replacement and secure remote-working procedures.
  • Maintain a call list and buyer notification process for severe disruption.
  • Record recovery objectives per service and test them at least annually.
  • Review continuity after incidents, major supplier change or a new contract.

Evidence

Business impact assessment, continuity plan, backup inventory, restoration test report, exercise record and follow-up actions.

5. Accessibility, Inclusion and Testing Policy

Standards

Public-sector digital services must be designed and tested against WCAG 2.2 Level AA and the Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations 2018 where applicable.

  • Use semantic HTML, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, sufficient contrast and accessible names.
  • Test with keyboard-only use, zoom/reflow, screen readers, automated checks and representative user needs.
  • Publish and maintain an accessibility statement for applicable public-sector services.
  • Log accessibility defects, business impact, interim measures and remediation dates.
  • Provide alternative formats and a clear accessibility feedback route when reasonable.

Procurement controls

No claim of full accessibility is permitted without conformance evidence. Exclusions or third-party limitations must be disclosed rather than hidden.

6. Quality Assurance and Delivery Policy

Controls

  • Translate contract and user requirements into an agreed scope, acceptance criteria and traceability record.
  • Use peer review and controlled versioning for code, documents, configurations and releases.
  • Separate development, test and production access where risk requires it.
  • Test functionality, security, accessibility, performance, compatibility and data quality.
  • Track defects by severity, owner and resolution; obtain acceptance before release.
  • Document handover, credentials, dependencies, support boundaries and maintenance responsibilities.

Change control

Changes affecting scope, cost, security, legal terms or delivery dates require recorded approval. Emergency changes must be retrospectively reviewed.

Evidence

Requirements matrix, plans, test reports, peer reviews, defect records, release approvals and handover checklist.

7. Anti-Bribery and Conflicts of Interest Policy

Policy statement

The company prohibits bribery, facilitation payments and improper influence in public or private procurement. Gifts or hospitality must be lawful, reasonable, declared and not capable of affecting decisions.

Procedures

  • Assess bribery risk proportionately; especially for tender intermediaries, agents, overseas activity and suppliers.
  • Perform documented due diligence on associated persons and relevant suppliers.
  • Maintain a gifts, hospitality and conflict-of-interest register.
  • Obtain approval before offering or accepting a material or public-sector gift or hospitality.
  • Never conceal payments, falsify records or offer personal benefit to a decision-maker.
  • Report suspected bribery to the director and seek police, SFO or legal advice where appropriate.

Training and review

Staff and contractors receive relevant briefing on joining and before higher-risk work. Risks and registers are reviewed at least annually.

Evidence

Risk assessment, due diligence, declarations, registers, training record and investigation log.

8. Equality, Diversity and Fair Work Policy

Policy statement

Recruitment, service delivery and workplace decisions must comply with the Equality Act 2010 and must not unlawfully discriminate or victimise anyone.

  • Use role-related recruitment criteria and consistent, documented selection decisions.
  • Consider reasonable adjustments and protected characteristic needs confidentially.
  • Prohibit harassment, bullying and discrimination, including by suppliers and clients.
  • Provide a fair route for concerns, complaints and appeals.
  • For public-sector work, support fair pay, equality duties and workforce data only where required and lawful.

Reporting

Concerns may be raised through the director or through the whistleblowing route. Retaliation is prohibited.

9. Modern Slavery and Responsible Supply Policy

Policy statement

The company has zero tolerance of modern slavery and human trafficking. Staff and suppliers must identify and report warning signs rather than conceal them.

  • Assess whether a Modern Slavery Act 2015 statement is required and publish it on time if applicable.
  • Risk-screen suppliers proportionately by location, sector, labour model and subcontracting.
  • Include lawful labour, audit and termination terms in contracts where appropriate.
  • Encourage confidential reporting and protect vulnerable workers.
  • Do not automatically terminate a worker or supplier when allegations arise; preserve reporting and remediation options.

Evidence

Supplier register, due-diligence checks, statement approval, contract terms and reported concerns.

10. Health and Safety Policy

Policy statement

The company will take reasonable and proportionate steps to protect staff, contractors and visitors under the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and supporting regulations.

  • Assess workplace and project risks before work starts and after material change.
  • Manage display-screen, lone-working, travel, electrical and fire risks in home or client locations.
  • Report and investigate accidents, near misses and work-related ill-health.
  • Provide instruction and equipment appropriate to assigned work.
  • Follow client-site induction, emergency and permit-to-work procedures.

Evidence

Risk assessments, training, equipment checks, incident records and client-site controls.

11. Environmental and Sustainable Delivery Policy

Policy statement

The company must prevent pollution, use resources efficiently and minimise avoidable waste and carbon impact where proportionate to operations.

  • Prefer secure remote collaboration and low-impact travel when feasible.
  • Buy and replace equipment only after need, lifecycle and reuse are considered.
  • Use authorised disposal or recycling routes for electronic and confidential waste.
  • Set supplier and project-specific requirements in the tender or contract rather than making unverified claims.
  • Obtain third-party assurance before claiming carbon neutrality or public procurement-specific environmental certification.

Evidence

Travel records, equipment disposal records, supplier evidence, energy or carbon methodology and improvement actions.

12. Whistleblowing and Speaking-Up Policy

Policy statement

Anyone working with the company may raise a genuine concern about suspected illegality, wrongdoing, danger or a serious breach of policy in confidence, without victimisation.

Process

  1. Raise the concern with the director or an alternative designated external adviser.
  2. Provide facts, dates and relevant records; avoid speculation or access to information beyond your duties.
  3. The concern will be logged, assessed and investigated proportionately with confidentiality where possible.
  4. The reporter will be told what can be communicated and be protected from retaliation.
  5. If the director is implicated, use the external reporting route or appropriate regulator.

External reporting

Where appropriate, concerns may be raised to the relevant regulator or the prescribed routes for whistleblowing under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998.

Evidence

Restricted concern register, investigation record, actions and outcome summary, with personal details minimised.
